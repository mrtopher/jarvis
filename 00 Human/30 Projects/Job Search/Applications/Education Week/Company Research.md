# Company Research - Education Week

## Snapshot
- **What they do:** America's most trusted K-12 education news, analysis, and opinion. Digital-first newsroom covering precollegiate education for school leaders, educators, policymakers, and the public. Publishing since 1981.
- **Size / stage:** Roughly 114 employees. Mature, mission-driven nonprofit media org (40+ years).
- **Funding / ownership:** Published by Editorial Projects in Education (EPE), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Revenue is diversified: subscriptions, paid content, advertising, and foundation grants. FY2025 revenue ~$19.8M (expenses ~$19.8M, net assets ~$34.5M), down from ~$26.3M in 2022. Notable funders: Carnegie Corporation of New York (general operating support), Annie E. Casey Foundation, Chan Zuckerberg Initiative.
- **HQ / locations:** Bethesda, MD.
- **Remote policy:** Hybrid (Bethesda-based).

## Products & business model
> Subscription-driven digital news operation (edweek.org) plus advertising and sponsored content, foundation-funded reporting projects, and events (e.g. the annual Leadership Symposium). The independence-through-diversified-revenue model is explicit editorial policy: no single funder should be able to sway coverage. Revenue mix means the CTO/CIO must balance editorial tooling, audience/subscription growth, and revenue systems at once.

## Tech stack
> Not disclosed in the posting. As a digital-first subscription publisher, expect a CMS/publishing platform, a subscription/membership and paywall system, marketing/analytics stack, CRM, and an ad/sponsored-content pipeline. The role explicitly favors a vendor/SaaS ecosystem over a large in-house engineering team, so the stack is likely composed of third-party platforms and implementation partners rather than bespoke software.

## Culture & values
> Mission-first, nonpartisan, independence-obsessed newsroom. Named a "Best Nonprofit To Work For" (2020). Editorial independence and public-interest service are core identity. Collaborative environment called out directly in the JD.

## Reputation
> Glassdoor ~4.2/5 across 24 reviews; 79% would recommend. Work-life balance 4.3, culture & values 4.0, career opportunities 3.4, compensation & benefits 3.4. Sentiment is positive overall (friendly, mission-driven), with the softer marks on pay and advancement typical of a mid-size nonprofit.

## Recent news
- Revenue has contracted from ~$26.3M (2022) to ~$19.8M (FY2025) - a real signal to probe on budget health and tech investment runway.
- Actively positioning for an "AI-enabled future," which is the stated reason this CTO/CIO seat exists.

## Green flags
- Direct CEO access and board exposure; genuine executive scope in a net-combined CTO+CIO seat.
- Strong culture and work-life-balance ratings; established, trusted brand.
- Digital-media / subscription-publishing domain is a direct match to Chris's Industry Dive / Pipeline 360 background.
- "AI-enabled future" mandate maps to Chris's core AI-strategy identity.
- Local (Bethesda hybrid) for a DC-area candidate; no relocation.

## Red flags
- Compensation band $180-220K sits below Chris's $250-300K target.
- Revenue down ~25% over three years; nonprofit budget pressure could constrain tech investment and the very roadmap this role owns.
- Role leans CIO/vendor-orchestration + IT/data-governance/cybersecurity policy rather than hands-on engineering leadership - lighter on the "build" work that is Chris's strength.
- Small internal team; more governance and partner management than team building.
- Comp & career-opportunity Glassdoor marks (both 3.4) are the weakest dimensions.
