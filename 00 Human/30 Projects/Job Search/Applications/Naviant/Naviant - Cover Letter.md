# Cover Letter – Naviant / VP of Technology and Solutions

Dear Mr. Carr,

I lead the implementation side of an AI consulting firm. My whole job is turning a client's AI strategy into systems that actually run. When I read that Naviant is moving toward an AI-first agentic automation firm, it read less like a job posting and more like a description of the work I do every week.

Naviant has spent nearly forty years earning trust as the partner who gets process right before the technology. That order matters. It is the same reason "AI-first" fails at most companies: they buy the tools before they fix the delivery. The VP of Technology and Solutions has to hold both, the forward move and the operational rigor that keeps a services business profitable while it changes. I have lived that tension.

At Pipeline 360 I owned technology strategy and delivery across a newly independent, hundred-person organization. I matured DevSecOps, CI/CD, and configuration-as-code into self-service. I also shipped a revenue-critical platform from zero to production in under ninety days, which protected an eight-figure revenue line without cutting corners on security. Earlier, I founded an engineering org and grew it from eight people to more than forty, built the career frameworks that kept them, and lifted retention fifteen percent. Today, at Dual Logic, I build agentic automation hands-on and advise CTOs on an AI-native delivery lifecycle, the exact evidence-based, guardrailed approach your posting calls for.

I will be direct about the one gap. My depth is in modern SDLC, cloud architecture, and applied AI, not in OnBase, ABBYY, or RPA specifically. I ramp on platforms fast, and the mandate here is the transition, not the legacy. That is where I am strongest.

I would welcome a conversation about what the first ninety days should really produce. I think we would agree on more than you might expect.

Sincerely,
Christopher Monnat
