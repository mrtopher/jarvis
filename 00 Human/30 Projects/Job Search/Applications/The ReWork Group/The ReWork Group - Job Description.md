# VP of Engineering — via The ReWork Group (client undisclosed)

> Source: supplied by candidate as pasted text. No URL was provided. Archived verbatim below. The ReWork Group is an engineering-leadership executive search firm; the actual employer (a commerce/logistics platform) is not named in the posting.

About the job
At The ReWork Group, we partner with high-growth startups and forward-thinking companies to build the future.

If you're a builder, hands-on, and ready to grow an engineering organization that defines what AI-first looks like in commerce, and then this is your next career move.

As the VP of Engineering, you'll take an org that's already strong and fast and push it toward an increasingly autonomous model of software delivery: AI does more of the build, engineers direct outcomes, and the distance from product idea to shipped software collapses. This does not make engineers less valuable. It makes them more valuable. When anyone can generate code, the advantage moves to the people with the judgment to decide what to build, and to the leaders who develop them.

You will own all of engineering: every product line, plus the shared platform and reliability functions everything else runs on.

What You'll Own:

AI-first engineering, for real. You own the mandate to make engineering AI-first end to end, not as tools bolted on the side but as the way the org works. You take a team that already uses AI well and push it toward an autonomous model of delivery. You bring conviction about where this is going and the track record to make it real, not a slide about it.
The org and its talent. You own the full engineering organization across every product line, plus platform and SRE. You lead and grow the leadership team, hire and develop leaders, and raise the bar. The goal isn't "world-class" in the abstract. It's an org that ships work others study, that top engineers seek out and are proud to belong to.
Technical strategy and architecture. You own the technical vision across the portfolio: system design, build-versus-buy, how we handle technical debt, and the standards that keep a fast-growing codebase healthy. You make the hard architectural calls and have the credibility to get them respected by your most senior engineers.
Delivery and execution. You own how fast and how predictably the org ships. You set the operating cadences and delivery metrics that keep multiple product lines moving, and you turn roadmap into shipped, high-quality software with Product. Throughput, quality, and predictability aren't trade-offs. A great AI-first org delivers all three.
Platform and shared services. You stand up and own the centralized platform the product lines depend on: the shared services and internal tooling, increasingly AI-powered, that let teams move fast without reinventing the core. You balance central leverage with product-team autonomy.
Reliability, performance, and security. You own uptime, performance, incident management, and SRE for a platform customers run their operations on. You partner with Applied AI and Internal Systems on security posture and with Data on the data platform, and you set the standard for operational excellence.

Who You Are:

You have a real, well-formed view on how AI changes engineering, and the receipts. You believe AI shifts engineers from writing code toward directing outcomes and makes the role far more product-oriented. You want engineers who own outcomes, not ticket-takers. And you've actually moved the numbers in a real org, not run a pilot.
You are technically deep and credible. You haven't drifted away from the technology. You go deep on architecture and the large-scale, event-driven, data-intensive, real-time distributed systems that power logistics and commerce. Your most senior engineers trust your judgment. Being personable is not a substitute for that.
You've built and scaled a high-performing org. You've led engineering at real scale, hundreds of engineers, at a high-growth software company: a venture-backed unicorn, a company that went public, or a comparable scaled platform. You've owned a multi-product or multi-team org, restructured it as it grew, and built the leadership layer beneath you.
You build teams the industry notices. You attract, grow, and keep exceptional engineers and leaders, and you build cultures others copy and try to poach from. You keep the ownership and urgency of a startup alive as the org scales.
You are a delivery leader. You've materially improved how fast and how reliably an org ships, through the right operating model, metrics, leaders, and increasingly AI, not heroics.
You've built platform foundations. You've stood up or scaled the internal platform and shared services that let product teams move fast, and you know how to balance central leverage with team autonomy.
