# Company Research – Churchstreeter and Crosse

## Snapshot
- **What they do:** Healthcare-exclusive talent and technology firm. They embed former healthcare executives (CEOs, COOs, CHROs, CMOs, CNOs) inside health systems to fix hiring, onboarding, retention, and leadership development, backed by a proprietary AI workforce-intelligence platform.
- **Size / stage:** Small, roughly 11-50 employees. Early-stage building a software product on top of a services business.
- **Funding / ownership:** Privately held, founder-led. No disclosed venture funding found. Revenue comes from exclusive, non-percentage-based consulting engagements (a waiting-list, one-client-per-space model).
- **HQ / locations:** Seattle, Washington.
- **Remote policy:** Role is "hybrid depending on organizational needs" with occasional travel. Hybrid base is unclear (likely Seattle-anchored). Confirm before committing.

## Products & business model
> Services-plus-software. The consulting arm partners inside healthcare organizations on workforce strategy. The technology arm is a proprietary, healthcare-built AI platform branded "CeCe" that turns workforce data into intelligence and decision support. This role exists to turn that platform into a commercial, enterprise-grade SaaS product: AI-powered recruiting, conversational and voice AI, LLM integrations, predictive and workforce analytics. Revenue model on the services side is exclusive annual engagements rather than percentage-based contracts. They also note serving healthcare and Indigenous organizations.

## Tech stack
> Not publicly documented. JD names the surface area: application development, AI/ML, cloud infrastructure, DevOps, QA, platform engineering, LLM integrations, conversational and voice AI. Greenfield enough that the incoming tech exec will likely set the stack.

## Culture & values
> Mission language: "Be bold. Be brilliant. Build what matters." Positioning is outcome-obsessed and embedded ("we join your team, apply data to root problems, and stay until the outcomes are measurable"). Claims 97% team retention and 100% client retention post-project. Founder story is legacy-and-entrepreneurship driven (inspired by the founder's great-grandfather).

## Reputation
> Too small and new to have a meaningful Glassdoor footprint. Public presence is company-authored (site, LinkedIn, The Org, Crunchbase). No independent employee reviews surfaced. Treat the glowing metrics as self-reported.

## Recent news
- No major press found. Presence is limited to company profiles and a healthcare hiring-event listing. This is an under-the-radar, founder-built firm, not a funded startup with a news trail.

## Green flags
- Founding-level tech seat. No CTO listed, so the hire owns architecture, stack, team, and product from the ground up. Real build-from-zero autonomy.
- Clean identity match for a player-coach technical executive who commercializes an AI platform.
- AI is central to the thesis (CeCe), not a bolt-on. The mandate is genuinely AI-native.
- Mission-driven, outcomes-first culture that rewards shipping measurable results.

## Red flags
- Domain distance. Healthcare workforce and HR/talent tech is not in Chris's background. The company is healthcare-exclusive.
- Small and services-funded. 11-50 people, no disclosed venture capital. Product runway depends on consulting cash flow, which is a different risk profile than a funded startup.
- Location ambiguity. Seattle HQ plus "hybrid depending on organizational needs" may not fit a remote candidate in Alexandria, VA. Confirm early.
- Comp undisclosed. No range published for a small, privately funded firm.
- Voice AI and conversational AI are named priorities; Chris has agentic/LLM depth but not a specific voice-AI track record.
