# Company Research – Patch

## Snapshot
- **What they do:** Hyperlocal news and information platform. Community news, events, classifieds, business listings, and local advertising across ~15,000 U.S. communities. 25M monthly users, ~3M active email subscribers, 85-person newsroom.
- **Size / stage:** Small but profitable operating company, private. Lean team relative to reach (85 in the newsroom; total headcount modest). Mature turnaround, not a startup.
- **Funding / ownership:** Owned by investment firm Hale Global. Originally an AOL property (AOL acquired the original Patch in 2009); spun out of AOL in 2014 as a joint venture with Hale Global, which turned it around to profitability. Not VC-backed in the traditional round-by-round sense.
- **HQ / locations:** New York roots; operates remote-first with employees and contractors across countries and time zones.
- **Remote policy:** Remote-first. Distributed team, flexibility across time zones, availability for significant production incidents.

## Products & business model
> Advertising-driven hyperlocal media. Revenue from local advertising plus events, classifieds, and business-listing products. The moat is scale of local coverage at low cost. Patch reached profitability after the AOL spin-out by running lean. The current growth story is AI: Patch used AI (PatchAM, AI-assisted newsletters) to expand hyperlocal coverage from ~1,100 to 30,000+ communities and stand up close to 1M newsletter subscribers across ~14,000 communities. The JD confirms a high-traffic website, iOS and Android apps, email, a custom CMS, custom digital products, payments, and data/advertising systems.

## Tech stack
> Per the JD: JavaScript/TypeScript, React, Next.js, Node.js on the modern side; Drupal/PHP as meaningful legacy. Infra spans AWS, Heroku, Fastly (CDN/edge), Pantheon (Drupal hosting). Data on GCP/BigQuery/GA4. Payments via Stripe. Identity via Auth0/Okta. Search via Typesense/OpenSearch. GitHub/Jira for SDLC. AI tooling: Claude, Bolt, Replit, Google Stitch. This is a classic modern-plus-legacy media platform: a Next.js/Node front and a Drupal/PHP core, glued with a CDN (Fastly) and search, being modernized while still running.

## Culture & values
> Mission-driven around local news ("antiquated thing everybody is giving up on," per CEO). Scrappy, lean, builder-first. The JD is explicit that this is "a builder's role, not a layer of management between the business and the technology" and wants someone "curious, direct, low-ego and willing to inspect the code, data or workflow before debating abstractions." Strong, separate Product function — the VP of Eng partners with the Head of Product but does not run Product.

## Reputation
> Glassdoor ~3.2/5 across 254 reviews; only ~42% would recommend to a friend. Work-life balance 2.9, culture/values 3.3, career opportunities 2.8. Positives: remote/flexible, good colleagues, funding to succeed. Negatives: management changes approach and priorities too frequently ("big push then change it a month or two in"); some historical reviews cite favoritism and exclusionary/DEI concerns. Priority thrash is the recurring theme and directly relevant to an engineering leader trying to set direction.

## Recent news
- AI newsletter expansion (PatchAM) scaled coverage to ~30,000 communities and ~1M subscribers — widely covered (Columbia Journalism Review, The Media Copilot, AdExchanger).
- CEO Warren St. John has been doing the media/conference circuit (SXSW, DMWF) positioning Patch as a profitable AI-driven local-news model.

## Green flags
- Profitable and independent — rare runway safety in this pipeline. Not burning VC money.
- Genuinely AI-forward with a real, shipped product (not slideware). Direct match to Chris's AI-native identity.
- Reports to the CEO, executive-team + Board exposure, owns eng + ad tech + Data + Site Quality — real senior scope.
- Remote-first (clean for a VA-based candidate).
- Comp $225-275K base + bonus + equity — at/near target band, and disclosed.
- Consumer-scale, high-traffic media platform with modern + legacy systems — Chris's exact Industry Dive lane.

## Red flags
- Priority thrash / frequent management direction changes on Glassdoor — the hardest part of setting technical direction here may be organizational, not technical.
- Lean team + multi-million (not tens-of-millions) operating portfolio — this is a hands-on, do-it-yourself seat, not a big-org command role. Good fit for a builder, thin on org-scale prestige.
- An existing "President, CTO Patch Labs / Consulting CTO for Patch.com" (Abraham Brewster) appears in org data — confirm how this VP seat relates to (or replaces) that fractional/consulting CTO arrangement before going deep.
- Historical DEI/exclusion complaints in older reviews — worth listening for in interviews.
- Hyperlocal ad-driven media is a structurally tough business; profitability is real but margins are lean.
