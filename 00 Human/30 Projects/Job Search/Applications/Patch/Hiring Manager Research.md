## Background
> Warren St. John is a journalist-turned-operator, not a career technologist. Born 1969 in Birmingham, AL; BA in English from Columbia. Reporter at The New York Times (2002-2008) and contributor to The New Yorker, Wired, Slate, the New York Observer. Author of two books (Rammer Jammer Yellow Hammer; Outcasts United). Joined Patch in January 2014 as editor-in-chief and has been CEO since 2016. He led Patch's turnaround to profitability under Hale Global and its recent AI-driven expansion. He describes himself with "pathological optimism" and frames Patch as "a little shop trying to do this antiquated thing everybody is giving up on" — hyperlocal local news.

## What they likely care about
- A **builder who ships**, not a manager who abstracts. The JD language ("inspect the code, data or workflow before debating abstractions," "not a layer of management between the business and the technology") is almost certainly his voice.
- **Business-legible technology.** As a non-engineer CEO, he needs a VP who translates technical plans into company priorities, capacity, and risk he can defend to the executive team and Board.
- **AI as a practical growth lever.** Patch's whole recent story is AI expanding coverage and products cheaply. He'll want someone who moves the strongest AI ideas safely into production, not slideware.
- **Reliability at consumer scale on a lean budget.** High-traffic site + apps + email + payments run by a small team; he needs velocity and reliability without a big org.
- **Mission fit.** He genuinely cares about local news surviving. Candidates who connect with that mission stand out.

## Connections & shared ground
