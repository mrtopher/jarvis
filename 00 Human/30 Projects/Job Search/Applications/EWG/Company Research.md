# Company Research – EWG (Environmental Working Group)

## Snapshot
- **What they do:** Nonprofit, nonpartisan consumer-health and environmental research/advocacy org. Publishes research and runs consumer-facing digital databases (Skin Deep for cosmetics, Food Scores, Guide to Healthy Cleaning, Tap Water Database, EWG Verified). Reaches ~30 million people a year.
- **Size / stage:** ~47 employees (small org, large public reach). Founded 1993 by Ken Cook and Richard Wiles. HQ Washington, DC, with offices/staff in CA and the Midwest.
- **Funding / ownership:** 501(c)(3) nonprofit. FY2024 total revenue $21.75M, expenses $18.64M, net income $3.12M, total assets $27.1M. ~90% of revenue from contributions ($19.6M). Financially healthy and growing (revenue well above the older ~$6.7M figure).
- **HQ / locations:** Washington, DC.
- **Remote policy:** Washington, DC hybrid preferred; remote considered for exceptional candidates. Telework flexibility listed in benefits.

## Products & business model
> Mission-driven nonprofit funded mostly by donations. The "product" is public-interest research surfaced through free consumer databases and apps (Skin Deep 130,000+ products rated against ~60 toxicity/regulatory databases; Food Scores; Healthy Living app; Tap Water Database). EWG Verified is a paid brand-certification program (a revenue and partnership line). The engineering org exists to run and modernize these data platforms at consumer scale, not to sell software.

## Tech stack
> Public signals point to a legacy content/data stack: WordPress and Drupal CMS, plus Facebook Pixel/Custom Audiences for marketing. Native iOS/Android Healthy Living app. The JD's own language ("reduce reliance on manual and spreadsheet-based workflows," "modernize the technology stack," "improve data ingestion, validation, and processing pipelines") signals aging systems, manual data ops, and a real modernization mandate. Cloud architecture, APIs, and data platforms named as required background.

## Culture & values
> Mission-first, science-driven, advocacy-oriented. Nonpartisan public-health framing. Small, flat teams where staff wear multiple hats. Founder-led (Ken Cook still President after 30+ years). Values transparency, consumer empowerment, and "not afraid to try something new."

## Reputation
> Glassdoor ~3.9/5 (small sample, ~27 reviews). Positives: good benefits, flexible/remote-friendly, ethical mission, Ken Cook well-liked, willing to innovate. Negatives: some "poor management by senior staff" notes, fast-paced and demanding, small-org resource constraints. Externally, EWG's science is sometimes criticized by industry groups (e.g. Skin Deep methodology debates) but the brand carries strong consumer trust.

## Recent news
- FY2024 financials show a healthy, growing org (net income $3.1M).
- Ongoing expansion of consumer databases and the Healthy Living app.
- JD itself is the notable signal: EWG is creating a senior engineering leadership seat to modernize its data/platform stack and bring in AI.

## Green flags
- Financially stable and growing nonprofit (not a turnaround; comfortable reserves).
- Comp band $180K–$300K is real and competitive for a nonprofit; top of band matches Chris's target.
- Clear, well-scoped modernization + AI mandate that matches Chris's actual strengths (legacy-to-modern platform, data pipelines, AI integration, hands-on player-coach).
- Newly created executive seat with no incumbent CTO to displace: greenfield leadership, high autonomy.
- Mission with broad public trust and 30M reach; meaningful work.
- Remote "considered for exceptional candidates," and telework flexibility is a stated benefit.

## Red flags
- Small engineering org (a handful of developers) and modest budget for tech; expect scrappy resourcing, not a large org to command.
- Legacy stack + manual/spreadsheet workflows means the hard part is organizational change, not just architecture.
- Nonprofit pace and consensus culture; some Glassdoor notes on senior-staff management friction.
- DC hybrid is "preferred"; a fully remote VA candidate must clear the "exceptional" bar (very doable, but confirm early).
- Founder-led for 30 years; a new exec must navigate long-tenured leadership and legacy decisions.
