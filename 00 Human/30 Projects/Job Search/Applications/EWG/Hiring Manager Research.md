## Background
> Ken Cook co-founded EWG in 1993 and has led it as President for 30+ years. Policy and advocacy background (agriculture and environmental policy, ex-Center for Resource Economics / The Conservation Foundation). He is the public face and brand of EWG, testifies before Congress, and hosts the EWG podcast. He is not a technologist, which is exactly why this seat is being created: EWG needs a senior engineering leader it can trust to own the technical strategy and translate it to a non-technical leadership and board.

## What they likely care about
- A hands-on leader who can modernize aging systems without breaking the consumer platforms millions rely on.
- Someone who can kill manual/spreadsheet data workflows and stand up real ingestion/validation pipelines.
- A trustworthy translator: can explain technology strategy to a non-technical President, board, and funders.
- Pragmatic AI adoption for consumer products, done responsibly (fits EWG's science-and-trust brand).
- Mission alignment; nonprofit stewardship of limited resources (build the right thing, not the expensive thing).
- A builder who is comfortable being both the strategy and much of the delivery in a small org.

## Connections & shared ground
> Mission-driven consumer health/environment. Chris's "AI strengthens people, it doesn't replace them" conviction maps cleanly onto EWG's human-centered, trust-first brand. Both value substance over hype (EWG is tired of industry spin; Chris is vocal about "AI slop"). Data-platform-to-production experience (Pipeline 360) mirrors the exact modernization mandate.

## Talking points
> Lead with the modernization-plus-AI thesis, framed for a non-technical President:
- "I've taken legacy, manual-heavy data operations and turned them into modern, automated platforms without disrupting the users who depend on them."
- "AI belongs in your consumer products where it earns trust, applied carefully, not as hype. That matches EWG's brand."
- "I can be your technical translator to the board and funders, and still be in the codebase."
- Emphasize small-org pragmatism and stewardship: build the right thing for the mission and the budget.
