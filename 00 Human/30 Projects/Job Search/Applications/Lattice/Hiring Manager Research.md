Sophie Roberts is the most senior engineering leader at Lattice and is named as "SVP of Engineering" on the Lattice About page. A Senior Director of Engineering leading 50+ engineers who "collaborates closely with the broader Engineering leadership team" almost certainly reports to her, either directly or through a VP who reports to her. ## What they likely care about
> Sourced primarily from a Code Climate webinar on data-driven engineering leadership. Note: some of those remarks were made while she was at Shopify, before Lattice. The themes are consistent with how her Lattice role is described.

- **Business-tied engineering.** She wants every engineer to understand how their work connects to business success, down to what an outage actually costs the business. At Shopify her teams sat in on customer interviews weekly so engineers could tie customer pain to the metrics they own.
- **Metrics with judgment, not metrics as autopilot.** She tracks four categories: operational/system health (SLAs, API failure rates, throughput), development metrics (time to commit, build time, build success rate), organizational health (team composition, retention, morale), and ability to hit goals. Her framing: the superpower is combining data with judgment and instincts, using metrics to drive curiosity rather than replace human judgment.
- **Transparency with the team.** She pushes leaders to share business metrics with engineers directly (for example through monthly business reviews).
- **People over tooling on AI.** She has written that AI does not make great companies, people do, and that real transformation comes from deep understanding, hard work, and deliberate change. She is bullish on AI but frames it as augmenting people.

## Talking points
> Specific things to mention in outreach or interview.

- Lead with a concrete example of tying an engineering team's work to a business metric and getting engineers to care about it. She interviews for exactly this instinct.
- Reference the four-metric mental model (system health, dev velocity, org health, goal attainment) without name-dropping it awkwardly; show you already think in those terms about a 50-person org.
- Speak to AI as augmentation, not replacement, especially in the context of Lattice's AI-native performance direction (they acquired Pando in Aug 2026 to push continuous, AI-native performance management). Tie AI features back to helping managers and employees, not automating them away.
- Show you can run customer-connected engineering: how you would get 50+ engineers across Performance, Engagement, and Goals to hear customer pain directly.
- If asked about org design, be ready to describe how you would partner with a peer product org (Vives owns product/design) rather than expecting to own product yourself.
