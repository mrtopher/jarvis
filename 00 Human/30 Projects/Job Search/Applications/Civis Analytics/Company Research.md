# Company Research – Civis Analytics

## Snapshot
- **What they do:** Data science technology and services firm. The Civis Platform is a unified data stack (warehouse, ELT/ingestion, identity resolution / customer 360, analytics and reporting, AI/LLM integration, governance), sold alongside professional services and consulting.
- **Size / stage:** Small and contracted. Roughly 43 employees as of early 2026, down sharply from a mid-100s to ~200 peak. Founded 2013.
- **Funding / ownership:** Private, venture-backed. About $73.5M raised across ~4 rounds. Board includes Eric Schmidt, Tim O'Reilly, and Christopher Olsen.
- **HQ / locations:** Chicago, IL. Remote-friendly with a hard state restriction (DC, IL, MD, NY, VA).
- **Remote policy:** Remote, but candidate must currently reside in DC, Illinois, Maryland, New York, or Virginia.

## Products & business model
> Origin story: spun out of the 2012 Obama campaign analytics team. Dual model. One side is the Civis Platform (data infrastructure PaaS). The other is a consulting/professional-services practice (survey and data science). The CTO role signals a strategic pivot: they are launching standalone AI products on top of the core data platform, and want a hands-on technical leader to own the cohesion between the two.

## Tech stack
> Not fully public. The platform is cloud-based (data warehouse + ELT + identity resolution + dashboards) with newer AI/LLM integration. Expect a modern cloud + distributed-systems footprint (the JD explicitly asks for it). Historically a heavy Python / R / data-science shop given the analytics roots.

## Culture & values
> Mission-driven: "objective, data-driven truth to organizational decision-making." Strong brand in the progressive nonprofit / advocacy / government analytics space. Values framed around data integrity and social impact.

## Reputation
> Glassdoor ~3.1/5 across ~115 reviews, 46% would recommend. Work-life balance rates well (~3.8). The recurring negative theme is leadership and management: several reviews describe a talent exodus, inconsistent comp/bonus/equity guidance, and HR that is slow to address issues. A common framing is "great consulting company, average tech company." Read this as a real, incoming-CTO turnaround signal, not a dealbreaker.

## Recent news
- Actively hiring a CTO and a VP, Sales & Services (both senior roles open at once suggests a leadership rebuild).
- General Counsel Laura Belmont departed for The Suite.
- Broad 2025–2026 industry backdrop of AI-driven contraction; Civis's own headcount has fallen substantially.

## Green flags
- Direct CEO report. Founder-CEO (Dan Wagner) still runs the company, so the CTO partners with the person who owns the vision.
- Genuine player-coach mandate for a hands-on, AI-product-building CTO. This is Chris's actual shape.
- Comp band ($225K–$300K) sits squarely in Chris's target range.
- Named, credible board (Eric Schmidt, Tim O'Reilly) and a strong mission-driven brand.
- Small org (~15 in the CTO's scope) means high leverage and real ownership from day one.

## Red flags
- Significant headcount contraction. A ~43-person company that peaked far higher is doing more with less; runway and stability are open questions.
- Glassdoor leadership/management complaints and a described talent exodus. The CTO would be walking into a rebuild.
- Two senior seats (CTO + VP Sales) open simultaneously can mean recent departures at the top.
- Hard location restriction (DC/IL/MD/NY/VA). Confirm Chris's state of residence qualifies before investing further.
- "Launch standalone AI products" from a services-heavy base is ambitious for a small, contracted team. Ask about budget, funding runway, and product-vs-services priority.
