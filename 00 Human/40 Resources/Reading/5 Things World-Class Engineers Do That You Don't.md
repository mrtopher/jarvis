# 5 Things World-Class Engineers Do That You Don't

## Summary
> A Life Engineered (Substack) piece by **Steve Huynh**, July 1, 2026. Thesis: elite engineers win not through raw talent or working harder, but through deliberate **compounding habits** - frequent growth assessment, influence multiplication, user-centric design, strategic differentiation, and turning failures into wisdom. Useful for Chris's team-leadership content pillar and for coaching/mentoring conversations.

## Key Points
- **1. Measure growth monthly.** Track new capabilities each month instead of waiting for annual reviews. "What did I do this month that I couldn't do last month?"
- **2. Amplify others' growth.** Move from individual output to scaling influence via mentorship and collaboration; personal throughput has a ceiling.
- **3. Hide complexity behind simple interfaces.** Absorb internal complexity so users experience simplicity ("packaged engineering") - customer experience over exposed architecture.
- **4. Refuse mediocrity.** Differentiate by working on non-consensus problems peers aren't chasing, not competing on the same high-visibility tasks.
- **5. Transform failures into lessons.** Convert personal mistakes into org-wide protections, then share the recovery narrative.
