# Blog Strategy: Engineering Leadership

## Executive summary

The blog is the authority home for one idea: how to lead engineering teams well in the AI era. It is not a traffic farm. It is a slower, deeper surface that earns trust from two readers at once, the hiring team looking at senior technical leaders and the executive deciding whether to bring in AI help. Substack stays the place where thinking gets written first. The blog is where the best of that thinking becomes a durable, linkable, citable reference piece.

## Strategic direction

- **Quality over velocity.** One strong pillar-grade post a month beats four thin ones. The differentiator is specificity, not volume.
- **Harvest, do not farm.** Most blog posts start as a Substack essay that already landed, then get expanded into a deeper reference version with structure, sources, and internal links. New original writing goes to Substack first.
- **Two readers, one post.** Every piece has to work for a hiring team (proof of judgment and range) and a prospective client (proof this person can lead their AI work). The engineering-leadership frame serves both.
- **Win on lived material.** 20 years of experience, live client engagements (anonymized with permission), real mistakes with real costs, and the current job search. Abstract leadership advice is everywhere. First-hand specifics are the moat.

## Audience

### Segment 1: Hiring teams and network
- **Role**: VPs of Engineering, CTOs, founders, technical recruiters, and the network that routes to senior technical roles.
- **Pain points**: Hard to tell a real AI-era engineering leader from someone repeating the hype. Worried about hiring a leader who cannot separate signal from noise.
- **Search behavior**: "AI engineering team structure," "how to lead engineers using AI," "engineering leadership AI adoption."
- **AI behavior**: Ask ChatGPT and Perplexity things like "how should engineering leaders adopt AI coding tools," "what changes about managing engineers with AI."
- **Content preferences**: Opinionated, evidence-backed, first-person. Proof of judgment over frameworks.
- **Buying stage**: Awareness to decision (a role or a referral).

### Segment 2: Prospective consulting clients ("Joe CEO")
- **Role**: Executives and operations leaders at organizations of roughly 50 to 1,000 people who want to adopt AI and lack senior technical leadership to do it well.
- **Pain points**: Uncertain where AI creates value and where the hype fails. Afraid of betting a quarter on the wrong tool or approach. No trusted technical voice in the room.
- **Search behavior**: "where does AI actually help my business," "AI adoption for mid-size companies," "should we build or buy AI."
- **AI behavior**: Ask assistants "how do I start adopting AI," "what AI projects are worth doing first."
- **Content preferences**: Plain language, a clear test or decision they can apply, honest limits.
- **Buying stage**: Consideration (evaluating whether to bring in help).

## Competitive positioning

### How we differentiate
- **First-hand over abstract.** Real client outcomes (anonymized with permission), real numbers, real mistakes with their cost. Most engineering-leadership content is generic advice with no receipts.
- **A decision the reader can run.** Every buyer-facing post ends with a test, checklist, or framework they can apply the same day.
- **Honest limits.** State where AI fails and where the hype breaks. This is the trust signal that separates the blog from vendor content.
- **Build in public.** The job search and the consulting build give a steady supply of current, specific material no competitor can copy.

## AI citation surface strategy

### On-site
- Lead each important section with its point, then the support. Assistants reuse self-contained passages.
- Keep entity terms stable. Say "AI-augmented engineering" consistently, do not rotate synonyms for key concepts.
- Use tables and checklists only where they add information, not for decoration.
- Add JSON-LD for BlogPosting, Person (Chris, with role and credentials), Organization (Dual Logic), and BreadcrumbList. Run `/blog schema` per post.
- Named-author byline on every post with a real bio that states the 20 years and the CTO role. This is the E-E-A-T anchor.

### Off-site (audience-first, no citation promises)

## Distribution
- **Primary**: LinkedIn (buyer and hiring-team audience). Work the handoff, re-hook the post, blog link in the first comment. This is the biggest unused lever.
- **Secondary**: Substack cross-reference from the original essay to the deeper blog version.
- **Tactical**: Reddit and YouTube only where the post genuinely fits and time allows.
- Weekends stay dark for LinkedIn (reach is near zero). Buyer tags, never engineer-bait.

## Content velocity (realistic for a solo operator in a job search)
- New blog posts: 1 to 2 per month, most repurposed from a Substack essay that already landed.
- Pillar pages: 1 per quarter (the anchor investment).
- Freshness updates: 1 per month on the highest-value post, only when facts or recommendations actually changed.
- Visuals: 1 useful chart or image per post where it adds information, on-brand for Joe CEO.
