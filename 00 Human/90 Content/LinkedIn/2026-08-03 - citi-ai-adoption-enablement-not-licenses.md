# LinkedIn - Citi AI adoption is about the 4,000, not the tool

## Angle
> The story in Citi's AI numbers is not the software. It is the 4,000 people whose whole job is helping everyone else use it well.

## Hook (first 2 lines)
Citi says AI is freeing up 100,000 engineering hours a week.
The number that actually matters is 4,000.

## Body
Citi says AI is freeing up 100,000 engineering hours a week.

The number that actually matters is 4,000.

That is the size of the internal network Citi built to help the rest of the company use AI well. Roughly 4,000 employees, its AI Champions and AI Accelerators, pulled from tech, operations, risk, and support, whose job is to help colleagues on their own teams actually use the tools.

The adoption is real. More than 70% of Citi's 182,000 employees now use firm-approved AI tools, over 80% of those with access use them regularly, and staff have logged more than 6.5 million prompts.

They earned that by treating adoption as a people problem and staffing it like one.

Most companies do the reverse: buy the tool, announce it, and wait for the numbers to move. Then they wonder why so many AI efforts show no return. (More on that study tomorrow.)

The tool is the cheap part. Teaching a large organization to use it well, under real controls, is the work. Citi paid for the work.

## Takeaway / CTA
If your AI rollout is a license and a launch email, you bought the easy 10%. Who owns the other 90% at your company?

Citi did not hire 4,000 new AI specialists. It pulled existing employees from tech, ops, risk, and support and made helping their teammates part of the job, peer to peer, inside the daily workflow. That is why adoption held.

Curious what others are seeing. If you have rolled out AI at any scale, who actually owns the day-to-day coaching?
