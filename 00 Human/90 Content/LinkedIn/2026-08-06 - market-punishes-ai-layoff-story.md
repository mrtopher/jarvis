# LinkedIn - The market punishes the AI layoff story

## Angle
> Investors punish companies that frame layoffs as an AI win harder than ordinary layoffs. If your only AI story is headcount you cut, that is a cost cut with a better narrative, not real value.

## Hook (first 2 lines)
Companies that blamed AI for their layoffs this year expected investors to cheer.
The market did the opposite.

## Body
Companies that blamed AI for their layoffs this year expected investors to cheer. The market did the opposite.

The Financial Times looked at tech firms that tied job cuts to AI. In the 30 trading days after the announcement, their stock trailed the Nasdaq by almost 10%. Firms that blamed something ordinary, a soft economy or over-hiring, trailed by about 4%.

So investors punish the AI layoff story harder than a plain one. A former Lululemon CIO made the same point in the New York Times this week. If the biggest thing AI does for your company is let you cut people, you found a cheaper way to do the same work and dressed it up as a strategy.

Real AI value looks different. The same team ships more. A support desk clears twice the tickets without losing quality, and engineers spend their week on the hard problems because the tool handled the routine ones. That kind of win shows up in revenue and output, not just a smaller payroll.

Amazon and Microsoft seem to get it. Both cut tens of thousands of roles this year, and both went out of their way to say AI was not the reason. They read the same tape.

## Takeaway / CTA
Run the same test on your own company. If the only way you can describe an AI win is the headcount you cut, you bought a cost cut. If you can point to more and better work from the people you kept, you changed how the business runs. Which one are you reporting?
