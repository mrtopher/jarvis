# LinkedIn - Own the model or rent it

## Angle
> Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta and 22 others signed a July 24 letter backing open-weight AI, and Jensen Huang broke his X silence to do it. The story for Joe CEO is not the policy fight. It is the choice underneath it: build AI strategy around models you can own and run, or stay locked to one vendor you rent from. Washington's decision changes which options he has.

## Body

Jensen Huang has never posted on X. His first post ever was to defend open AI models.

He and Satya Nadella signed a letter with 23 other companies today. Here is why a CEO should care.

On July 24, twenty-five American tech companies put their names on one letter. Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta, IBM, Dell, Palantir, Hugging Face, Mistral, Y Combinator, Andreessen Horowitz.

Their argument is short. The US keeps its AI lead by building an open model ecosystem. Locking it behind one best system loses that lead.

Open-weight models are the ones you can download, run, and shape to your work. No single vendor holds the keys.

The letter landed a week after a Beijing company released Kimi K3, an open model good enough to reset what people expected a downloadable system could do.

Washington is deciding right now whether to restrict open models. That decision changes your options.

If open models stay available, you get real choice. You can run AI on your own terms, keep sensitive data in house, and avoid being locked to one provider's price and roadmap.

If they get restricted, you are back to renting from a handful of large vendors on their terms.

Most companies I talk to have never asked whether they should own a model or rent one. This letter is the reason to start asking.

## Takeaway / CTA
Are you building your AI strategy around models you rent, or models you can own?

One thing I tell clients: "rent vs own" is not all or nothing. Most should rent a frontier model for hard, general tasks and run a smaller open model in house for the repeatable, data-sensitive work. The mistake is defaulting to one vendor for everything and never running the comparison.

Which side is your company leaning, rent or own?
