## Body
Most job posts for engineering leaders are still screening for 2019. They are about to hire the wrong person for an AI-era team.

I have read a lot of these postings this year. Most rank the same things they always did. Years in a language. A list of frameworks. How many people you have managed. Those made sense when the hard part of the job was writing the code.

The hard part moved.

When AI can produce a working draft of almost anything, the leader's job is judgment. Knowing which problem is worth solving. Catching the confident-wrong output before it reaches a customer. Deciding what the team should refuse to build.

What I would screen for instead:

- Can they tell a genuinely good AI output from one that only looks right?
- Have they actually built with the current tools, or only read about them?
- Can they lead a team of builders to decide together, now that everyone can build?
- Do they know where AI helps and where it quietly makes the work worse?

None of that fits a keyword filter. So the resume that scores highest is often the one most stuck in the old model, and the person who could actually lead the team never makes it past the screen.

## Takeaway / CTA
If you are hiring an engineering leader this year, read your own job post again. Is it screening for the person who can type, or the person who can judge?
