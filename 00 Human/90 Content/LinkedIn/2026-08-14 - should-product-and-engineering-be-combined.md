# LinkedIn - Should product and engineering be combined?

## Angle
> The wall between product (what to build) and engineering (how to build it) made sense when building was slow. AI made building fast, so the handoff is now the slow part. Combine the team, not the titles.

## Hook (first 2 lines)
Product decides what to build. Engineering decides how.

## Body
Product decides what to build. Engineering decides how. That wall is now the slowest thing in your company.

For years the split made sense. Product wrote the spec, engineering built it, and work crossed a wall between them. When building was slow and expensive, that handoff was worth its cost. You planned carefully because a mistake took months to unwind.

AI changed the math. A small team can now build a working version of an idea in the time it used to take to write the spec for it. Once building gets that fast, the handoff becomes the bottleneck. Every trip between "what" and "how" is time the market no longer gives you.

Combining them into a single job is the wrong fix. A strong product mind and a strong engineer are still different skills, and pretending one person is always both is how you get shallow products and sloppy code.

Combine the team, not the titles. Put the person who owns the outcome and the people who build it on the same side of the table. Let them decide together, ship together, and hear from the same customer in the same week. The wall comes down while the two crafts stay intact.

Most companies that feel slow right now are not slow at building. They are slow at deciding, because the deciding and the building happen in different rooms with a document carried between them.

## Takeaway / CTA
Look at your last shipped feature. How many times did it cross the wall between product and engineering before a customer ever saw it? That number is your real cycle time.
