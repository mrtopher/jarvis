## Body
I've spent twenty years writing software, but this week I decided to switch things up and become a student again. I just got my Certified AI Engineer credential from AI Makerspace, and I'm pretty excited about it. It's not every day you get to learn new things and challenge yourself, but I'm looking forward to seeing where this new skill takes me.

I always say the same thing to my clients every week. If you want to lead a team, you need to have gone through the same changes yourself. The AI engineering stack is completely different from what it was just a year ago - we're talking about retrieval, agents, evaluations, and production tradeoffs. But here's the thing: reading about all this stuff is not the same as actually building it and seeing it fail. You can't just learn about it in a book or a lecture, you need to get your hands dirty and try it out for yourself. That's the only way you'll really understand how it works, and how to make it work for your team.

So I built. I shipped the projects, hit the confident-wrong output, and learned where these systems actually fail instead of where the demos say they do.

What really stayed with me were two key points. Creating the model itself is actually the simpler part. It's everything that comes after - the plumbing and evaluation - that's where the real challenge lies. The engineers who truly succeed are the ones who carefully verify the output before it ever reaches the customer.

That's the same thing I've always been interested in, but now I'm looking at it with a new set of tools.
