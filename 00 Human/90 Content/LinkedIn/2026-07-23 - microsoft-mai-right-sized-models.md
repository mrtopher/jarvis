## Body

Microsoft just proved the biggest AI model is not always the right one.

They trained a smaller, specialized model for GitHub Copilot and Excel. The results should make every CEO rethink their AI budget.

In VS Code, their small model hit a 10% higher code accept rate than GPT-5.4 Mini and Claude Haiku 4.5.

Developers came back more often, 6% more likely to return day over day versus one competitor and 11% more likely versus the other.

It used 10% fewer tokens to do it.

The Excel version matched a much larger model on common tasks at lower cost. It even ran on older H100 and A100 chips instead of demanding the newest hardware.

Fit is what drove these wins.

Most companies reach for the largest, most expensive model by default. They pay a premium for capability they never use on the task in front of them.

A smaller model trained on your actual workflow can beat the giant one where it counts, and it runs faster and cheaper.

Right-sizing the model to the job is the strategy most AI budgets are missing.

## Takeaway / CTA
Where in your business are you paying for a model far larger than the job requires?

## Seed Comment
The full breakdown from Microsoft is worth a read if you want the numbers: https://microsoft.ai/news/hill-climbing-mai-models-for-github-copilot-and-excel/

One thing I keep telling clients: model selection is a budget decision, not just a technical one. The default move is to pick the biggest name and move on. That works, but you pay for capability the task never uses.

A quick test I use: name the top three tasks your AI actually runs all day. Then ask what the smallest model is that clears the bar on those. Most teams have never run that exercise.

What's the one AI task in your business you'd trust a smaller, cheaper model to handle?
