# LinkedIn - Felt faster, measured slower

## Angle
> AI makes a strong engineer stronger only when you measure the outcome, not the feeling. A team that feels fast while shipping slow is the expensive kind.

## Hook (first 2 lines)
A study put experienced developers on AI tools and timed them.
They felt 20% faster. They were 19% slower.

## Body
A group called METR ran a randomized trial. Take experienced developers, give some of them AI coding tools, and time real work on their own projects.

The developers expected AI to make them about 24% faster. It made them 19% slower.

It gets stranger. Even after finishing, they still believed AI had sped them up by 20%. The feeling and the stopwatch disagreed by almost 40 points.

The lost time went into double-checking. The tool produced a lot, fast, and a senior engineer could not just trust it. So they read it, tested it, and fixed the subtle wrong parts. That is the tax on output you cannot rely on.

Two honest caveats. This used early-2025 tools, which have improved since. It also studied seasoned engineers on code they knew cold, where AI helps least.

The lesson survives both. AI makes a strong engineer stronger when you measure the outcome, not the vibe. A team that feels faster while shipping slower is the most expensive kind, because everyone is sure things are going great.

## Takeaway / CTA
Are your AI gains showing up in something you can count, or only in how fast the work feels? One of those pays the bills.

## Notes
- Peg, attributed: METR, "Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity" (randomized controlled trial, July 2025). 16 experienced OSS devs, 246 tasks, Cursor Pro + Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet. Predicted +24% faster, actual -19% slower, still self-reported +20% after. Primary slowdown factor: low AI reliability -> time spent verifying output. METR published a Feb 2026 note that they are redesigning the experiment (question still open). arxiv 2507.09089.
- Stated both real caveats (early-2025 tools; expert devs on familiar code) so the post is honest, not cherry-picked. Ties "AI makes engineers stronger" to "AI slop vs quality."
