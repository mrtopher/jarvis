## Body
Most companies deploying AI agents have not answered a basic question. When the agent gets it wrong, who is on the hook?

Deloitte found roughly three in four organizations plan to run agentic AI within two years. Very few have the governance to match. Everyone wants the workforce that never sleeps. Almost no one has decided who answers for it.

Look at the companies doing it seriously. Citi just launched a platform to run agents across the bank, and what stands out is the controls around them: who can deploy one, what it can touch, who reviews it, who owns the outcome. They built the accountability before they scaled the autonomy.

That order matters. An agent that can email customers, move money, or change a record has stopped being a tool. It acts on your behalf. If no human name sits behind it, you have handed authority to something you cannot hold responsible.

None of this means wait. Decide the boring things first. For every agent you deploy, name the owner, the limits, and the review. Do that and you can move fast without waking up to a mess no one saw coming.

## Takeaway / CTA
For every AI agent you are about to turn loose, can you name the human who answers for it? If you cannot, that agent is running without an owner, and that is how the incident starts.
