# LinkedIn - Why 95% of AI pilots return nothing

## Angle
> AI pilots fail on choices, not capability. Wrong use case, no success criteria, a pilot nobody can grade.

## Hook (first 2 lines)
95% of corporate AI projects show zero return.
The failure is almost never the model.

## Body
MIT looked at hundreds of corporate AI efforts last year and found 95% showed no measurable return. Zero impact on profit or loss.

The number is contested, and it should be. It came from a few dozen interviews, not audited books. Treat it as directional, not gospel.

The direction holds up everywhere else you look. PwC's 2026 CEO survey found 56% of chief executives saw neither more revenue nor lower costs from AI. Gartner predicted at least a third of generative AI projects would be abandoned after proof of concept by the end of 2025.

So the tools work in the demo and the money never shows up. That is not a technology problem.

The same MIT work found partnered builds succeeded about twice as often as in-house ones, and that quiet back-office automation returned more than the flashy sales and marketing bets most budgets chase. The pattern is about choices: which use case you picked, whether you defined success up front, whether anyone can grade the result.

If you cannot say in one sentence what a pilot has to prove and by when, you do not have a pilot. You have a science experiment on the company card.

## Takeaway / CTA
Before you fund the next AI project, write the one sentence that says what it must prove and by when. If you cannot write it, that is your first problem to solve.
